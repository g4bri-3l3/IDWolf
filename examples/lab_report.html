🤖AI summary and remediation plan

The Active Directory domain test.com exhibits a severely compromised security posture with a hygiene score of 21/100, indicating widespread administrative misconfigurations and exposure to domain-wide takeover. Most critically, multiple non-default user accounts hold direct DCSync replication rights on the domain head, allowing immediate full domain compromise. In addition, the environment suffers from unconstrained delegation on a non-DC computer, missing LAPS deployment, and weak password and auditing policies. Immediate remediation must focus on revoking unauthorized DCSync rights, enforcing secure authentication and auditing controls, and hardening delegation properties.

Can the findings be chained into full compromise?

Verdict: likely · starting from: any domain user

The domain can be compromised immediately due to non-default principals holding direct DCSync rights. Additionally, NTLM relay vulnerabilities and unconstrained delegation provide alternative direct escalation paths to Tier 0.

Direct DCSync Takeover
dcsync_rights
A standard domain user or compromised principal with DCSync rights can immediately pull all password hashes from Active Directory, achieving full domain compromise. Fix by stripping the DS-Replication-Get-Changes-All extended right from aprilette.madelyn.

Objects: TEST\aprilette.madelyn
NTLM Relay to LDAP via WebDAV Coercion
webclient_not_disabled → ldap_signing_not_required
An attacker can coerce a domain controller via WebDAV coercion to authenticate to an attacker-controlled listener, relaying the NTLM challenge to LDAP since signing is not enforced, and creating a new Domain Admin account. Fix by disabling the WebClient service and requiring LDAP signing.

Objects: Domain Controllers

Quick wins

  • Disable the Print Spooler service on domain controllers to prevent coercion.
